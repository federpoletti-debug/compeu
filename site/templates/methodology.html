How the CompEU is calculated

The full method, versioned. Any change produces a new version; past fixings keep the version they were calculated with and are never recalculated.

1. What the index measures

The on-demand list price of one GPU-hour, in euro excluding VAT, offered in {{ data.methodology.scope }} by cloud providers that are sovereign under rule {{ data.rule.id }}. A second family of indices measures the same thing for US hyperscalers operating in Europe, so the two can be compared on one scale.

2. Sources

Each provider is read from its own public source, never from a third-party comparison site. Where the provider publishes an API, the API is used; otherwise its public product page. Collection runs {{ data.schedule | lower }}. Every price is stored with the time it was read, the exact source URL and the SHA-256 hash of the verbatim records it came from; those records are kept in the repository.

The rule looks at control, not at server location: the US CLOUD Act (18 U.S.C. § 2713) reaches data held by a provider under US jurisdiction "regardless of whether such communication, record, or other information is located within or outside of the United States". The approach follows France's SecNumCloud qualification (ANSSI) and the direction of the EU's cloud certification scheme (EUCS) and the Commission's Cloud and AI Development Act proposal. The rule zone is the EU's 27 member states and Switzerland.

If a fact needed by the rule is unknown or contested, the provider is under_review and excluded from both baskets. Statuses are computed by code from registry facts; a status is never typed by hand.

4. Eligible offers

  • Pricing model: on-demand. Spot, dynamic, reserved and committed prices are recorded but not used.
  • Location: {{ data.methodology.scope }}. An offer published as a single catalogue price counts only when the registry marks that catalogue as the provider's European price list, with the reason written down.
  • GPU: matched to a canonical model (for example all H100 variants: SXM, PCIe, NVL). Names that match no model are recorded as unmapped and never guessed.
  • Evidence level: Level 1 (public list price) and above. Levels are recorded on every price and never merged.

5. From offers to a fixing

  1. For each provider, take the prices from its latest successful collection on or before the fixing day. Offers the provider no longer lists are gone; old prices are never carried forward.
  2. A provider whose latest successful collection is more than {{ data.methodology.staleness_days }} days old is excluded and listed as stale.
  3. Provider quote: {{ data.methodology.provider_quote }}. One provider, one vote.
  4. Currency: non-euro prices are converted with the ECB euro reference rate of the latest TARGET business day on or before the observation day. A rate older than six days is refused.
  5. Index value: the {{ data.methodology.aggregation }} of the quotes, rounded to four decimals.
  6. Data sufficiency: below the minimum number of constituents the fixing is published as insufficient_data, with no value. Nothing is estimated, interpolated or carried forward.
  7. Finality: a fixing is written once per index per day and never revised.

6. Coverage

The coverage measure counts the sovereign providers that publicly list at least one frontier GPU (Hopper generation or newer, or AMD MI300 or newer) on demand in Europe, over all sovereign providers in the registry, including those that publish no prices. Providers without public data count as not listing, so missing data never makes coverage look better.

7. The ledger

Prices, runs and fixings are stored as append-only text files in the public repository. Each run record stores the hash of the price lines it added and the hash of the previous run; fixings are chained the same way. An automated check rejects any change that edits or deletes an existing line. Anyone can verify the whole history with uv run compeu verify.

8. Governance and limits

Phase 1 publishes an informational index, not a regulated benchmark. The methodology is written to follow the IOSCO Principles for Financial Benchmarks (2013) so that it can be submitted for authorisation later without rewriting history. List prices are not transaction prices: discounts, commitments and availability are not captured until Level 2 and Level 3 data arrive.
