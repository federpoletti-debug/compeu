Rule {{ data.rule.id }} · in force since {{ data.rule.effective_from }}

Control, not location

The US CLOUD Act lets US authorities require a US-controlled provider to hand over data wherever it is stored. So the test looks at the company, the same way France's SecNumCloud and the EU's draft cloud certification do. A provider is sovereign only if all three answers are yes.

1 · Seat

Registered in the EU or Switzerland

The contracting company's legal seat is in one of the EU's 27 member states or in Switzerland.

2 · Control

Owned and controlled there too

The ultimate controlling owner is also European. A German subsidiary of a US group fails here.

3 · Reach

Out of reach of the CLOUD Act

No parent subject to the US CLOUD Act or an equivalent law with extraterritorial access to data.

A provider whose control is contested, such as a joint European and non-European shareholding, is marked under review and left out of both baskets until the facts are settled. Every status is computed from the registry; none is typed by hand. The UK is outside the rule, as is any provider whose ultimate owner is outside the EU and Switzerland.

Methodology {{ data.methodology.id }}

How a fixing is made

  1. Collect. Every day, read each provider's public price list: its API where one exists, its product page otherwise. Every price is stored with its source, time and a hash of the verbatim response.
  2. Classify. Apply rule {{ data.rule.id }} to each provider's legal facts. The rule version is stored on every price.
  3. Select. Keep on-demand offers located in {{ data.methodology.scope }}. Spot, reserved and global marketplace prices are shown but never counted.
  4. Quote. Each provider gets one vote: its cheapest price per GPU-hour, in euro at the ECB reference rate of the observation day.
  5. Fix. The index is the median of the quotes. Below the minimum number of fresh providers it is not published. A fixing, once written, is never revised.

Three levels of evidence

Level 1 public list price, read from the provider's own source. Level 2 price confirmed by the provider. Level 3 real transaction data. Every price carries its level; levels are never mixed into one number. All prices today are Level 1.

Roadmap

From a price index to a market

Phase 1 · live

The index

Daily public prices, a sovereignty test, an append-only ledger. Free to read and to cite.

Phase 2 · planned

Capacity desk

Pool demand from European buyers who need sovereign GPUs and negotiate capacity with neoclouds. Transaction data from the desk becomes Level 3 input.

Phase 3 · subject to licensing

A reference price

Cash-settled contracts on compute prices need a licensed venue and an authorised benchmark administrator. The methodology already follows the IOSCO benchmark principles so that history built today stays usable.
