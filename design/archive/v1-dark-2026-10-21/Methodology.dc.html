How the CompEU index is calculated

The whole method, versioned. A change produces a new version; past fixings keep the version they were calculated with and are never recalculated.

{{t.label}}
1

What the index measures

The on-demand list price of one GPU-hour, in euro excluding VAT, offered in the EU, the EEA or Switzerland by cloud providers that are sovereign under rule sovereignty/1. A second family of indices measures the same thing for US hyperscalers operating in Europe, so the two can be read on one scale.

2

Sources

Each provider is read from its own public source, never from a comparison website: its API where one exists, its product page otherwise. Collection runs every day at 05:20 UTC. Every price is stored with the time it was read, the exact source and the fingerprint of the verbatim records it came from.

3

The sovereignty rule

A provider is sovereign when all three answers are yes: its legal seat is in the EU or Switzerland; its ultimate owner is there too; and no parent is subject to the US CLOUD Act or a similar law with access to data stored abroad. The rule looks at control, not at where the servers are, in line with France's SecNumCloud and the direction of the EU's cloud certification scheme and its Cloud and AI Development Act proposal.

When a fact the rule needs is contested or unknown, the provider is marked under review and kept out of both baskets. Statuses are computed by code from the provider registry. Nobody types a status by hand.

Try the rule on a provider, or on your own cloud
4

One provider, one price

A provider can list the same GPU in many sizes. CompEU divides each offer by its number of GPUs and keeps the cheapest, so a provider with forty sizes still gets one vote. Here are Verda's H100 offers from the run of 20 October. Pick one to see the arithmetic.

5

From dollars to euro

Prices in another currency are converted with the European Central Bank's euro reference rate of the latest business day on or before the day the price was read. A rate older than six days is refused, and the price waits rather than being converted with a stale rate.

6

From quotes to a fixing

For each provider CompEU uses the prices of its latest successful collection on or before the fixing day. Offers the provider no longer lists are gone; old prices are never carried forward. The index is the median of the quotes, rounded to four decimals. Below the minimum number of providers (three sovereign, two hyperscalers) the fixing says not published and has no value.

A provider whose last successful read is more than three days old drops out until it answers again. Move the slider to see when.

7

Coverage

The coverage measure counts the sovereign providers that publicly list at least one frontier GPU (Hopper generation or newer, or AMD MI300 or newer) on demand in Europe, over all sovereign providers in the registry, including those that publish no prices. Missing data counts as not listing, so it never makes coverage look better. On 20 October 2026: 3 of 9.

8

The ledger

Prices, runs and fixings are append-only text files in the public repository. Each run stores the fingerprint (SHA-256) of the price lines it added and of the run before it; fixings are chained the same way. An automated check rejects any change that edits or deletes an existing line, and anyone can re-run the whole check.

$ git clone https://github.com/federpoletti-debug/ceresio-index
$ uv run compeu verify
ledger OK
9

Governance and limits

Phase 1 publishes an informational index, not a regulated benchmark. The method follows the IOSCO Principles for Financial Benchmarks so it can be submitted for authorisation later without rewriting its history. List prices are not transaction prices: discounts, commitments and availability are not captured until Level 2 (confirmed by the provider) and Level 3 (real transactions) data arrive. Every price carries its level, and levels are never merged into one number.
